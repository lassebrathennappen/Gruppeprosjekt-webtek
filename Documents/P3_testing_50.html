1. Administrative details

Client: TODO: client name, TODO: position in the organisation.

The website belongs to OPA!, a Greek restaurant in Trondheim city centre (Munkegata 1) serving traditional Greek food with a modern twist. The site lets visitors read about the restaurant, browse the food and drinks menu, find events and special offers, and reserve a table.

2. Testing document

Our main method was live, observed usability testing. Each tester was given seven tasks, read aloud one at a time so that later tasks did not give away earlier answers. We did not explain how to navigate the site, and we did not help during the tasks. For each task we recorded whether it was completed, the time spent, the path the tester took, any hesitation or misclicks, and direct quotes. Testers also rated each task from 1 (very easy) to 5 (very difficult).

Tasks

  1. Find the opening hours.
  2. Find out if there are any drinks that tempt you.
  3. Find out where the restaurant is located.
  4. Book a table.
  5. Read the history of the restaurant.
  6. Find out if the restaurant has any special offers or events.
  7. Find the menu for the event.

After the tasks, testers were asked follow-up questions on design, navigation, content, usability, accessibility (keyboard navigation, 200 % zoom, contrast) and responsiveness.

5. Feedback summary

All three testers completed all seven tasks without help (21 of 21 tasks, 100 %). Almost every task was rated 1, which suggests the overall structure and navigation work well.

Navigation

Testers mostly went straight to the right page from the main menu. About us was found in 1–10 seconds, and the events page in 5–16 seconds. The menu still worked on the iPad when the menu items wrapped onto a new line.

The opening hours caused the most searching. Two of three testers looked elsewhere first: T1 checked the home page and About us before finding them in the footer (rated 3, the highest rating in the test), and T3 found them in the footer but expected them on the Contact page.

“Contact is nice too. I think the opening hours should be there.” – T3 (translated)

Content

The location was easy to find because the address appears on the home page, in the footer and on the Contact page, which two testers commented on positively. The events page received praise for its clear heading.

The event menu confused one tester. T2 first went to Menu and found the grill menu there, and only later realised it was the same menu as on the events page. He suggested naming it “Special grill menu” on the menu page and explaining that it is a deal, and adding a heading or a “Menu” button on the events page that jumps down to the menu. T1 and T3 found it right away.

Usability

Booking a table took the longest on all devices (39 s to 1 min 32 s), yet every tester rated it 1. This suggests the form is long rather than confusing. T3 hesitated at the email field. Both laptop testers praised the form:

“I like that I can see what I have chosen and edit it.” – T1 (translated)

“I love that I can jump between text fields with the arrow keys.” – T3 (translated)

Design and responsiveness

In iPad portrait orientation, the image next to the food on the events page became heavily cropped and zoomed in, and the menu could have taken up more of the page. In the footer, the opening hours sat very close to the logo. The menu options on the Menu page adapted well. Otherwise the layout looked good.

6. Our response

We have not changed the website after testing. Below is what we would do to address the feedback.

Show the opening hours where people look for them

Two of three testers expected the opening hours somewhere other than the footer. We would add them to the Contact page and show them near the address on the home page. The footer can keep them as well, since it is consistent across pages.

Make the event menu clearer

We would rename the grill menu on the Menu page to “Special grill menu” and add a short note that it is an event deal, with a link to the events page. On the events page, we would add a “See the menu” link that jumps to the menu section using an anchor (id), plus a clear heading above the menu.

Speed up the booking form

The form is rated easy but is slow to complete. We would review whether every field is necessary, explain why we ask for an email address (for example to send a booking confirmation), and add autocomplete attributes so the browser can fill in name, email and phone. We would keep the parts testers liked: the summary of choices and the ability to edit them.

Improve the tablet layout

For narrow screens we would adjust the events image with a media query, for example by changing the aspect ratio or object-position so it is less cropped, and give the menu more width. In the footer we would make the logo smaller and add more spacing between the logo and the opening hours.

Limitations

With a small number of testers, a problem experienced by one person (such as the event menu) may or may not be common. We would want to test the changes with new testers, including more guests over 40 and people who use assistive technology such as screen readers, before drawing firm conclusions.
